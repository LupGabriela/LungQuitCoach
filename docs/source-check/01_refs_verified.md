## 3. Claim checks

**[25] Tammemägi 2013: PLCOm2012 horizon, development cohort, AUCs: CONFIRMED (with a wording caveat)**
- Horizon: the abstract defines risk as the probability of a lung-cancer diagnosis during the 6-year study period.
- Development cohort: the Methods say the model was developed in the PLCO **control** group of smokers. Table 2 is titled "36,286 Control Participants Who Had Ever Smoked". Validation used the PLCO **intervention** group (Table 3: 37,332 smokers) and NLST.
- Caveat: the abstract itself says the model was "developed and validated" with data from the 80,375 ever-smokers in the PLCO control and intervention groups. If the report cites 36,286, attribute it to the Methods/Table 2, not the abstract.
- AUC: the abstract gives 0.803 in the development set and **0.797** in the validation set. The **0.701** NLST AUC (95% CI 0.689–0.712) appears in the **Results**, not the abstract.
- URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3929969/

**[16] Oken 2011: participants aged 55–74: CONFIRMED.** The abstract describes 154,901 participants aged 55 through 74 years. https://experts.umn.edu/en/publications/screening-by-chest-radiograph-and-lung-cancer-mortality-the-prost/

**[15] NLST 2011: 20% relative reduction: CONFIRMED.** The abstract gives a relative reduction in lung-cancer mortality with low-dose CT of 20.0% (95% CI 6.8–26.7). https://scholars.houstonmethodist.org/en/publications/reduced-lung-cancer-mortality-with-low-dose-computed-tomographic-/

**[22] Scanlon 2000: FEV1 decline 62 vs 31 mL/yr: CONFIRMED.** The abstract says that after quitting, FEV1 improved in the first year by an average of 47 mL (2%). After that, sustained quitters declined at half the rate of continuing smokers: 31 ± 48 vs 62 ± 55 mL (mean ± SD). The abstract calls this a rate of decline but does not print "/yr" next to the numbers; FEV1 was measured annually. https://experts.umn.edu/en/publications/smoking-cessation-and-lung-function-in-mild-to-moderate-chronic-o/

**[17] Parkes 2008: quit rates 13.6% vs 6.4%: CONFIRMED.** Independently verified 12-month quit rates were 13.6% in the intervention group (lung-age feedback) and 6.4% in the control group (raw FEV1 feedback). https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2267989

**[31] Wies 2026: 126 schools, 9,797 students, no significant reduction: CONFIRMED (add nuance).** The trial enrolled 126 German secondary schools and 9,797 grade 6/7 students. The between-group difference in smoking was not statistically significant (p = 0.228). However, the abstract also says the intervention reached a predefined threshold for clinical relevance (NNT 93). The report should say "no statistically significant reduction" rather than "no effect". https://www.cris.fau.de/publications/365967743/

**[3] Clair 2019: little evidence that biomedical risk assessment increases quitting: CONFIRMED.** The authors conclude there is little evidence about the effects of biomedical risk assessment as an aid for smoking cessation. Spirometry and carotid ultrasound, the most promising, rest on moderate-certainty evidence and narrowly missed significance; genetic-marker feedback rests on low-certainty evidence with no benefit detected. They state that further research is likely to change the conclusions. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6434771 ; https://www.cochrane.org/CD004705

**[9] Jeblick 2024: mostly correct/complete, but some errors: CONFIRMED.** The abstract says most radiologists agreed the simplified reports were factually correct, complete and not potentially harmful. It adds that there were nevertheless instances of incorrect statements, missed relevant medical information and potentially harmful passages. https://epub.ub.uni-muenchen.de/116334/1/s00330-023-10213-1.pdf

**[12] Kumarasamy 2025: projection method and horizon: CONFIRMED.**
- Method: each participant's PLCOm2012 risk was projected forward, increasing age, smoking duration and years since quitting by one per year. COPD, personal and family cancer history, education and BMI were held constant, smoking status was assumed unchanged, and current smokers kept their baseline intensity.
- Second scenario: complete, sustained cessation after the first evaluation, with no relapse.
- Horizon: from first evaluation **until age 80**, so the number of years varies by participant. A 10-year window from baseline is also reported as a summary.
- Threshold: PLCOm2012 6-year risk ≥ 1.51% (the ILST threshold).
- Population: 4,451 ILST participants aged 55–80 who were ineligible at baseline (risk < 1.51%); median age 61.
- Findings: about half were projected to reach eligible risk before age 80. The abstract reports that cessation reduces a projected proportion from 68.7% to 24.9% by age 70. That figure came through a summarised fetch, so re-read the abstract before quoting it.
- URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12322426/

**[30] Weiss 2023, CXR-Lung-Risk: CONFIRMED.**
- What it predicts: risk of lung-disease mortality from a single chest X-ray. The AIM lab page lists COPD, interstitial lung disease, emphysema and lung cancer.
- Training data: PLCO, 147,497 CXRs from 40,643 individuals.
- Testing: three cohorts with 15,976 individuals in total: a held-out PLCO subset (about 10.5k), NLST heavy smokers (about 5.5k) and the Boston Lung Cancer Study, BLCS (about 1k, histologically confirmed lung cancer).
- Caveat: only NLST and BLCS are truly external. The held-out PLCO subset is an internal test set.
- Sources: abstract numbers from https://cris.maastrichtuniversity.nl/en/publications/deep-learning-to-estimate-lung-disease-mortality-from-chest-radio ; PLCO training and cohort names from https://aim.mgh.harvard.edu/cxr-lungrisk , https://www.auntminnie.com/imaging-informatics/artificial-intelligence/article/15633482/ai-estimates-lung-cancer-mortality-on-xrays and the official README mirror https://github.imc.re/AIM-Harvard/CXR-Lung-Risk (approximate cohort sizes come from the README).
